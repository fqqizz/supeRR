1. Problem statement

Round 1 asks for a data-driven system that measures how batters and bowlers perform in high-pressure match situations compared with their usual performance. The required outputs are two ordered lists: the ten best-performing batters under pressure and the ten best-performing bowlers under pressure.

The definition of pressure and the analytical method are left to the participant. This report therefore explains the selected match situations, performance measures, comparison rules and evidence behind the final order. These are historical performance rankings, not player-retention or auction recommendations.

2. Pressure definition

The pressure state is determined immediately before a delivery. A batting delivery is labelled pressure if either condition applies:

  • Death phase: at least 75% of scheduled legal balls have elapsed. In a standard 20-over innings, this corresponds to overs 16–20.
  • Batting-resource pressure: five or more wickets have already fallen before the delivery.

The conditions form a union. A delivery meeting both is counted once. Bowling is evaluated in the death phase only. Exact required run rate, score margin and match closeness are not used because the supplied fields do not support a reliable reconstruction.

3. Dataset and preparation

The analysis uses the supplied ball-by-ball data and supporting competition, team, player, match and playing-XI records. The primary scope is IPL 2022–2026 and T20I; other domestic T20 competitions are excluded.

There are 828 matches in the final scope: 364 IPL and 464 T20I. After match and innings filters, 193,372 eligible delivery rows remain, including 57,330 batting-pressure deliveries. Match state is reconstructed in delivery order so that pressure is assigned using pre-delivery information.

4. Batting model

The baseline batting score gives equal weight to four dimensions. Metrics are converted to percentiles within format and observed batting role.

Pressure output

Pressure strike rate = 100 × pressure runs ÷ pressure balls faced
Boundary-ball rate = 100 × pressure balls producing four or six ÷ pressure balls faced
Output = 0.70 × pressure strike-rate percentile + 0.30 × boundary-ball-rate percentile

Observed batting positions are grouped into 1–2, 3–4, 5–7 and 8–11. Extended 12+ positions are retained descriptively but are not used as a standard peer cohort. Role-level scores are combined by pressure-ball exposure; at least 80% scoreable role coverage is required.

Adaptability

Peer percentile of the role- and format-adjusted difference between pressure strike rate and normal-context strike rate.

Dismissal-risk control

Inverse percentile of empirical-Bayes dismissal probability per pressure ball. Shrinkage reduces the influence of very small samples.

Match consistency

Inverse percentile of the IQR of match-level pressure strike rates. A match must contain at least five pressure balls; at least five qualifying matches are required.

Baseline batting score = 0.25 × Output + 0.25 × Adaptability + 0.25 × Risk control + 0.25 × Consistency

Eligibility requires at least 60 pressure balls, pressure observations in at least five matches, five qualifying consistency matches, a normal-context baseline and at least 80% scoreable role coverage in each format. Five weight configurations are tested: equal dimensions; output-led (40% output); adaptability-led (40% adaptability); risk-control-led (40% risk control); and consistency-led (40% consistency), with the remaining weights adjusted to total 100%.

5. Bowling model

Bowlers are compared within format and Pace/Spin peer groups. The score combines death-phase effectiveness and match consistency.

Death wicket rate = 100 × bowler-credited death wickets ÷ legal death balls
Effectiveness = 0.85 × adjusted death wicket-rate percentile + 0.15 × known-boundary control percentile
Bowling score = 0.65 × Effectiveness + 0.35 × Consistency

Consistency is the inverse percentile of the IQR of match-level death wicket rate. A qualifying match contains at least six legal death balls; at least five such matches are required. Eligibility also requires at least 36 legal death balls in each format and a non-death baseline.

The known-boundary measure is partial. It is not official economy rate, total runs conceded or complete dot-ball percentage. Three bowling weight configurations are tested: 65/35 balanced, 80/20 effectiveness-led and 50/50 consistency-led.

6. Reliability and cross-format ranking

Competition-season environment references are used when the group contains at least 120 pressure balls or legal death balls, 10 distinct players and three matches. Otherwise, the calculation falls back to the broader format-and-role reference. The adjustment is: adjusted rate = raw player rate × leave-one-player-out format-role reference ÷ leave-one-player-out environment-role reference. The player's own observations are removed from the peer totals.

Empirical-Bayes estimates stabilise dismissal and wicket probabilities. Match-cluster bootstrap intervals are retained as supplementary uncertainty evidence. Percentiles are relative peer positions, not probabilities of future performance.

Three format blends are tested: equal (50/50), IPL-priority (70/30) and exposure-weighted (square-root exposure, capped between 30% and 70% per format). Only players eligible in both formats enter the combined list.

Batting uses five component-weight configurations crossed with three format blends (15 scenarios). Bowling uses three component configurations crossed with the same three blends (9 scenarios). Final rank is the mean rank across scenarios.

Top-ten stability = 100 × top-ten appearances ÷ total scenarios

8. Limitations

  • Exact required run rate, score margin and match closeness are not reconstructed.
  • Bowling boundary control is a partial proxy, not official economy or total runs conceded.
  • Knockout observations are retained as diagnostics but are not weighted into the final score because qualifying samples are sparse.
  • Percentiles are relative peer positions, not probabilities, causal effects or future forecasts.
  • The combined rankings exclude players who do not meet eligibility rules in both formats.

The final rankings should be read alongside pressure exposure, format-specific evidence and scenario stability.
