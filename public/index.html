RESEARCH NOTE / 05

Methodology

Definitions, calculations, eligibility rules, ranking procedure and limitations used in this analysis.

Download full PDF ↓Open HTML methodology ↗

The sections below describe the calculations used to produce the final lists. The downloadable report gives the full written methodology and player evidence.

01
CORE PRESSURE DEFINITION

Pressure definition

For batting, a delivery is labelled pressure when at least 75% of scheduled legal balls have elapsed or five or more wickets have already fallen before the delivery. The two conditions are combined as a union. For bowling, the analysis evaluates the death phase only. The label is based on pre-delivery information.

02
BATTER MODEL

Four weighted components.

  • Output: 70% adjusted pressure strike-rate percentile + 30% pressure boundary-ball-rate percentile.
  • Adaptability: adjusted pressure-minus-normal strike-rate percentile.
  • Risk control: inverse percentile of empirical-Bayes dismissal probability.
  • Consistency: inverse percentile of match-level pressure strike-rate IQR.
Balanced score = 25% × (Output + Adaptability + Risk + Consistency)
03
BOWLER MODEL

Death-phase effectiveness and consistency.

  • Effectiveness: 85% adjusted death wicket-rate percentile + 15% known boundary-event control percentile.
  • Consistency: inverse percentile of match-level death wicket-rate IQR.
  • Peer groups: format × Pace/Spin.
  • Deep-lineup test: retained as corroboration, not a second weighted signal.
Balanced score = 65% × Effectiveness + 35% × Consistency
04
CONTEXT NORMALISATION

Compare like with like.

Batters are grouped by observed position: 1–2, 3–4, 5–7 and 8–11. Extended 12+ positions are descriptive only. Bowlers are grouped by Pace/Spin. Competition-season benchmarks are used only with at least 120 pressure balls or legal death balls, 10 players and 3 matches; otherwise the broader format-role benchmark is used. The player is excluded from its own peer reference.

Adjusted rate = raw player rate × leave-one-out format-role rate ÷ leave-one-out environment-role rate
05
RELIABILITY

Minimum evidence and uncertainty.

Empirical-Bayes shrinkage pulls unstable rates toward the format prior. Match-cluster bootstrap intervals are retained as uncertainty evidence. Eligibility gates apply separately in each format.

BATTER 60+ ballsBOWLER 36+ legal balls
06
CROSS-FORMAT RANKING

Three format-blending assumptions.

Only players who meet the evidence rules in both IPL and T20I are eligible for the final combined list. Format scores are blended under equal, IPL-priority and evidence-weighted approaches.

50 / 5070 / 30Exposure weighted
07
FINAL ORDER

Mean rank across tested scenarios.

Batting uses five dimension-weight configurations × three format blends = 15 scenarios. Bowling uses three configurations × three blends = 9 scenarios. Final order is the mean scenario rank; stability is the proportion of scenarios in which the player remained top ten.

Stability = Top-10 appearances ÷ Total scenarios

WHAT THIS MODEL DOES NOT CLAIM

No exact required run rate. Current score, score margin and required run rate are not reconstructed because extras data is incomplete.

No official bowling economy. Known batter-boundary events are only a partial control proxy, not total runs conceded or true dot-ball rate.

No future prediction. Percentile scores are relative to observed peers; they are not probabilities, causal effects or guaranteed future performance.

No auction recommendation. These are historical pressure-performance rankings only. Availability and franchise fit are outside this Round 1 question.
